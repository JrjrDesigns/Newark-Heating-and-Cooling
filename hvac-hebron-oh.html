Heating and cooling for Hebron homes

Hebron sits in southern Licking County where Route 79 meets I-70, a few minutes from Buckeye Lake. The village itself has older homes close to the center and newer houses around the edges, and the surrounding township mixes subdivisions, farmhouses and homes on larger lots. That means a wide spread of fuel types: natural gas in town, propane and all-electric heat as you get farther out.

What we see around Hebron

  • All-electric homes heated by electric furnaces or older heat pumps, with winter electric bills to match.
  • Propane furnaces in homes beyond the gas lines, where efficiency matters more because propane costs more per unit of heat.
  • Furnaces in crawl spaces and utility closets, where filters are easy to forget and hard to reach.
  • Older village homes with add-on air conditioning and ductwork that was never designed for it.
From the field

We see a lot of Hebron-area homes with an electric furnace that has been doing all the heating since the house was built. Switching that electric furnace to a properly sized heat pump, with the electric strips kept as backup, is one of the few upgrades that routinely pays for itself in a few winters. We run the numbers with your actual bills before recommending it.

Winter near the lake

The low ground around Buckeye Lake and the south side of the county can run a few degrees colder than town on still nights, and damp crawl spaces are common. Moisture is hard on furnace cabinets and ductwork in crawl spaces, and it shows up as rust, mold smells and loose duct connections.

From the field

When we replace a crawl space furnace near Hebron, we almost always find ductwork that has pulled apart somewhere under the house. Heat that should be warming the living room is warming the dirt. Reconnecting and sealing those ducts during the replacement often makes a bigger difference than the new furnace does.

Propane furnace or heat pump?

For Hebron-area homes beyond the natural gas lines, the choice is usually between a propane furnace, a heat pump, or both. A propane furnace delivers hot air on the coldest nights and is simple and reliable, but propane costs more per unit of heat than natural gas. A cold-climate heat pump heats efficiently through most of the winter and cools in summer. Paired together, the heat pump carries the load on mild days and the propane furnace takes over when it gets truly cold.

The right answer depends on your propane price, your electric rate and how the house is built. We compare the options with your real numbers before recommending anything. Read more on heat pumps and repair or replace.

Questions we hear

HVAC questions from Hebron homeowners

Do you service propane furnaces around Hebron?

Yes. We repair and install propane furnaces throughout the Hebron area. If a propane tank ran completely empty, the propane supplier needs to leak-test and relight the system first; then we make sure the furnace starts and runs correctly.

Is a heat pump worth it for an all-electric home?

Usually, yes. Compared with an electric furnace or baseboard heat, a properly sized heat pump uses far less electricity for the same heat. We compare against your actual bills before recommending it.

Can you work on a furnace in a crawl space?

Yes. Crawl space furnaces are common around Hebron. Access is tighter, but we repair and replace them regularly and check the ductwork while we are under there.

How quickly can you get to Hebron?

Hebron is within our regular service area, and same-day service is typical. No-heat calls are answered 24/7 at (740) 527-0222.

What does it cost to replace an electric furnace with a heat pump?

Most heat pump installations run about $5,000 to $9,000 depending on size and whether ductwork needs changes. See the cost guide.
