Heating and cooling for Alexandria homes

Alexandria sits on Route 37 between Granville and Johnstown, a village of a few hundred people with St. Albans Township’s farms and rural properties all around it. The village itself is mostly older homes: front porches, plaster walls, and heating systems that have been replaced two or three times since the house went up. Outside the village, homes sit on wells and propane, on lots big enough that neighbors are a field away.

What we see around Alexandria

  • Older village homes with ductwork retrofitted into houses built for stoves, so some rooms have always run cold.
  • Propane furnaces on the township roads, where natural gas has never reached.
  • Aging central air added in the 90s and 2000s, now at the end of its life.
  • New builds creeping west from Granville and New Albany, bringing heat pumps and tighter houses with them.
From the field

In the older homes right in the village, the second floor is the tell. Ductwork that was squeezed into wall cavities decades ago barely moves air up there, and owners assume that is just how the house is. It usually is not. Sometimes the fix is as small as opening up a choked return; sometimes a single mini-split head solves a bedroom that ducts will never reach properly.

Small village, full coverage

Villages this size get skipped. Companies from Columbus quote big trip windows, and homeowners end up waiting days for a repair that takes an hour. Alexandria sits squarely inside our county-wide territory: same pricing, same 24/7 emergency coverage, same trucks that are already working Granville and Johnstown every week.

From the field

A propane furnace west of Alexandria once taught us to always ask about the tank first. The furnace would run, shut down, run again; the owner was bracing for a big repair. The tank gauge read fine, but the regulator outside was icing up in freezing rain and starving the furnace. A propane-side fix, not a furnace-side one, and a much smaller bill than anyone expected.

Getting a St. Albans Township home ready for winter

  • On propane, keep the tank above a third through the cold months and get on automatic delivery if your supplier offers it.
  • Have the furnace tuned up in the fall, especially if the nearest help used to be an hour away. Catching a worn igniter in October beats losing heat in January.
  • If rooms over the porch or at the ends of duct runs never warm up, ask us about it during the tune-up. It is usually fixable without new equipment.

Questions we hear

HVAC questions from Alexandria homeowners

Do you actually come out to Alexandria, or is it an extra-charge area?

We cover Alexandria and St. Albans Township at the same prices as everywhere else in Licking County. Our trucks work Granville and Johnstown weekly, so you are on the way, not out of the way.

Our house is on propane. Do you work on propane furnaces?

Yes, every week. We repair and replace propane furnaces and can convert or configure equipment for propane. If a tank runs dry, your propane supplier must leak-test and relight first; after that, we handle anything on the furnace side.

The upstairs of our old village house barely gets warm. Can that be fixed?

Almost always. The usual causes are undersized supply runs and choked returns from old retrofits. Fixes range from duct corrections to a single ductless mini-split head for the stubborn rooms.

How fast can you get here for a no-heat call?

No-heat calls are triaged first, county-wide, and answered 24/7 at (740) 527-0222. We will give you an honest arrival window when you call, not a guess.

Can you service the newer homes being built around the village?

Yes. New builds around Alexandria increasingly come with heat pumps and tighter envelopes, and we handle warranty-age equipment, correct setup and seasonal maintenance on all of it.
