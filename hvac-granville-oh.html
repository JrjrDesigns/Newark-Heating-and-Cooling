Heating and cooling for Granville homes

Granville is one of the most distinctive places we work. The village center around Broadway is full of nineteenth-century homes, many beautifully kept and some with original plaster, trim and radiators that owners want preserved. Around Denison University and out into Granville Township, newer homes on larger lots are often bigger and more complex, with two systems, zoning and high-efficiency equipment. Both call for careful work.

Older village homes

  • Boilers and radiators. Many older Granville homes are heated with hot water or steam. The heat is excellent, but there is no ductwork for central air.
  • Plaster walls and finished trim that should not be cut into to run new ducts.
  • Tight, stone-foundation basements that make furnace replacement a careful carry-in job.
  • Outdoor equipment placement. In the village's architectural review district, where outdoor units go and how visible they are can matter. We plan placement with that in mind and help you check what the village requires.
From the field

A lot of the older Granville homes we work in have radiators the owners love and no air conditioning at all. Two or three well-placed ductless mini-split heads cool the bedrooms and main living space in summer without touching a plaster wall inside, and they give a second source of heat in the spring and fall so the boiler can stay off longer.

Newer Granville homes

Outside the village, newer homes often have two systems, one for each floor, or a single system with zoning dampers. Those setups are comfortable when they are balanced and frustrating when they are not. Common calls include a zone that never reaches temperature, a damper motor that has failed shut, or a second-floor system that was sized off the first-floor one.

From the field

Homes on the hillsides around Granville catch a lot more wind than those in the valley, and it shows up as uneven heating on the windward side of the house in January. Before recommending a bigger furnace, we look at air sealing and duct balance, which usually solves more than extra BTUs would.

Rental homes near Denison

Granville has a good number of rental houses near Denison University and the village center, and landlords have different priorities than owner-occupants: reliability, fast response when a tenant calls, and equipment that survives filters not being changed as often as they should be. We work with landlords to schedule maintenance between tenants, put filter sizes on the furnace where they are easy to see, and install equipment with good parts availability so repairs are quick.

If you manage a rental in Granville, a fall furnace tune-up before students return from break is the cheapest insurance against a no-heat call from a tenant in January.

Questions we hear

HVAC questions from Granville homeowners

Can you add air conditioning to an older Granville home with radiators?

Yes. Ductless mini-splits are usually the best fit because they need only a small hole through the wall for each indoor head. In some homes, a small-duct high-velocity system is another option.

Do I need village approval for a new outdoor unit?

It depends on where your home is and what is visible from the street. In Granville's architectural review district, exterior changes can require review. We help you plan placement and check with the village before installation.

Do you work on boilers?

Our focus is furnaces, heat pumps, air conditioning and mini-splits. For boiler-heated homes, we commonly add cooling and supplemental heat with mini-splits alongside the existing boiler.

My Granville home has two HVAC systems. Can you service both?

Yes. We service multi-system and zoned homes regularly, and we can check the balance between systems during a tune-up.

Do you protect floors and finishes during work?

Always. We use floor protection from the entry to the work area and take extra care in older homes with original woodwork.
