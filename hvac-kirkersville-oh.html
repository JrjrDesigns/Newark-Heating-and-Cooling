Heating and cooling for Kirkersville homes

Kirkersville is one of those Licking County villages you know from Route 40: a few streets of homes along the old National Road between Etna and Hebron, with Harrison Township’s farms and rural lots around it. Many of the houses here go back generations, built long before central heat, and the ones that are newer tend to be ranches and modest two-stories from the decades when the village last grew.

What we see in Kirkersville

  • Older village homes where the furnace lives in a cellar with stone walls and a low ceiling, and getting parts in and out is half the workout.
  • 1960s–80s ranches with furnaces and ACs on their second or third replacement cycle.
  • Propane systems on the township roads outside the village limits.
  • Commuter households where everyone works in Columbus or at the Etna warehouses, and service has to fit around real schedules.
From the field

The cellars in the older Kirkersville homes keep us honest. Low ceilings, stone walls, and a furnace that was clearly lowered in before anyone measured the stairs. When we quote a replacement in one of these houses, we measure the access first, because picking equipment that actually fits down the steps matters as much as picking the right BTUs.

A village this size deserves a straight answer

When something breaks in a village of a few hundred people, the frustrating part is rarely the repair; it is getting anyone to commit to showing up. Kirkersville sits minutes off our regular routes through Hebron and Etna, so a call here books like a call in town: a real arrival window, a price before work starts, and 24/7 emergency coverage when the heat quits at night.

From the field

A no-cool call here one July turned out to be a condenser buried in cottonwood fluff from the trees along the creek. Ten minutes with a coil rinse and the system was back to full capacity, and we showed the owner how to hose the fins gently each June. Not every fix is a part; sometimes it is just knowing what the trees in your part of the county do to an AC.

Getting a Kirkersville home ready for winter

  • Have the furnace tuned up in the fall, especially if it is past ten years old. Village homes on their third system often have quirks worth catching early.
  • Change the filter before the season starts, and check it monthly once the furnace is running daily.
  • If part of the house never warms up, mention it when you book. Duct fixes in these older homes are often small and cheap.
  • On propane outside the village, keep the tank above a third and set up automatic delivery.

Book a fall tune-up, or call (740) 527-0222 if you already have a problem.

Questions we hear

HVAC questions from Kirkersville homeowners

Is Kirkersville really in your service area?

Yes, fully. We run Route 40 between Etna and Hebron every week, so Kirkersville is on the way, not a special trip. Same prices as everywhere else in Licking County.

Our furnace is in an old cellar with tight stairs. Is replacement a problem?

No, but it takes planning. We measure the access before quoting so the new equipment actually fits the space and the stairs. It is a routine part of working in the older villages.

Can you schedule around a Columbus commute?

Yes. First-of-the-day and late-afternoon slots exist for exactly this reason, and for true emergencies the phone is answered 24/7 at (740) 527-0222.

My AC runs but barely cools by late June. What is that?

Around here, check the outdoor coil first. Cottonwood and grass clippings mat the fins and choke airflow. If a gentle rinse does not bring it back, refrigerant or a weak capacitor is next, and we can diagnose it the same day in most cases.

Do you handle propane furnaces outside the village?

Yes. Propane furnaces, dual-fuel heat pumps and full replacements on the township roads are regular work for us. If your tank runs dry, the supplier leak-tests and relights first; we handle everything on the equipment side.
