Why sizing matters more in Ohio humidity

An air conditioner has two jobs: lowering the temperature and pulling moisture out of the air. It removes humidity only while it runs long enough for the indoor coil to get cold and drip. An oversized unit cools the house fast, shuts off, and leaves the air cool and clammy. That is the most common complaint we hear from Licking County homeowners with newer systems, and it almost always traces back to a unit that was sized by copying the old one or by a square-foot rule of thumb.

We size every system with a load calculation based on your home's insulation, windows, orientation and air leakage. The right size is frequently smaller than what was there.

From the field

Two-story homes in Pataskala and Johnstown subdivisions often have one system for both floors, with the upstairs running five or six degrees warmer all summer. A bigger AC does not fix that. Balancing dampers, an added return upstairs, or a variable-speed system does. We look at the ductwork before we recommend equipment.

Matching the system

Your air conditioner works together with the indoor coil and the furnace blower. A new outdoor unit paired with an old, mismatched coil will not deliver its rated efficiency and can shorten compressor life. When we replace an AC, we replace the coil to match and set the furnace blower speed for the new system. If your furnace is close to the end of its life too, replacing both at once saves on labor.

New systems installed today use newer refrigerants such as R-454B, and all new central air conditioners must meet federal SEER2 efficiency minimums. Higher-efficiency, two-stage and variable-speed models cost more up front but run quieter, control humidity better and use less electricity through a long Ohio summer.

Consider a heat pump instead

A heat pump is an air conditioner that can also run in reverse to heat your home. When an AC needs replacing and you have a gas furnace in decent shape, installing a heat pump instead costs somewhat more and lets the heat pump handle heating on mild fall and spring days, with the furnace taking over when it gets truly cold. Read more on our heat pump page.

Installation day

A standard AC replacement takes most of a day. We recover the old refrigerant properly, set a new pad if the old one has sunk or cracked, install the outdoor unit and matching indoor coil, run a new line set when the old one is undersized or damaged, pull a deep vacuum, charge the system to the manufacturer's specifications and test it under load. We pull the permit where required and haul everything away.

From the field

In older Newark homes where the AC was added long after the house was built, we often find the line set running through a basement window well or a wall that has since been finished over. Rather than splice onto thirty-year-old copper, we plan a clean new route. It is a small cost that prevents the most common source of refrigerant leaks.

Getting a price

Estimates are free and written, with model numbers, efficiency ratings and warranty terms so you can compare quotes fairly. Typical Licking County prices are listed in our HVAC cost guide. Fall and winter are the quietest months to replace an air conditioner, and scheduling is easiest then.

Questions we hear

AC replacement questions

How much does a new central air conditioner cost in Licking County?

Most central AC replacements run about $3,800 to $7,500 installed, depending on size, efficiency and whether the line set and pad need to be replaced. See the cost guide for ranges by size.

What size AC do I need?

It depends on your home, not just its square footage. We run a load calculation that accounts for insulation, windows, sun exposure and air leakage. The result is often smaller than the unit you have now.

Can I replace my AC in the fall or winter?

Yes, and it is often the best time. We can install and test a system in cool weather, and scheduling is much easier than in July.

Do I need to replace my furnace at the same time?

Not always. If your furnace is in good shape, we match the new AC to it. If it is over about fifteen years old, replacing both together usually saves money on labor.

How long does a central air conditioner last?

Most last about 12 to 17 years in central Ohio. Keeping the outdoor coil clean and changing filters regularly makes a real difference.
