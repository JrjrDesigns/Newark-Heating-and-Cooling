Heating and cooling for New Albany homes

New Albany is mostly in Franklin County, but part of it extends east into Licking County, and that is the side we serve most often from our base in Newark. The community is known for its Georgian-style architecture, and its homes tend to be large, newer and equipped with more complex heating and cooling than a typical house: two or three separate systems, zoning dampers, high-efficiency furnaces and variable-speed equipment.

What we see in New Albany homes

  • Multiple systems serving different floors, with one often working much harder than the others.
  • Zoning systems with damper motors that fail quietly and leave a zone stuck open or closed.
  • Communicating and variable-speed equipment that needs technicians comfortable with its controls and diagnostics.
  • Attic air handlers serving upper floors, where condensate drains and secondary pans need regular attention.
  • Whole-home humidifiers and media filters that are only as good as their maintenance.
From the field

In larger homes with an air handler in the attic, the most expensive problem we see is not a failed part. It is a secondary drain pan that filled up because the primary drain clogged and the float switch was never installed or had been bypassed. A ceiling stain on the second floor is usually the first sign. We check both drains and the safety switch on every attic unit we service.

Keeping complex systems balanced

Multi-system homes are comfortable when every system is sized and balanced for the part of the house it serves. When one is short on charge, has a failed damper or is fighting a thermostat setting on another floor, the whole house feels off, and the energy bills climb. We service every system in the home in the same visit and look at how they work together, not just whether each one turns on.

From the field

A common New Albany call: the second floor is warm every summer evening, and the homeowners assume the upstairs unit is too small. More often, the upstairs system is fine and the attic around its ductwork is over 130 degrees. Sealing and insulating that ductwork, or relocating a return, often fixes what a larger unit would not.

Maintenance for multi-system homes

A home with two or three systems has two or three times as many filters, drains and safety switches to keep track of. The most reliable approach is to service every system in the same visit, twice a year: heating in the fall and cooling in the spring. Each visit covers the equipment and the parts of the home that support it, like humidifier pads, media filters, zone dampers and attic drain pans.

We keep a record of the equipment, filter sizes and anything we flagged, so the next visit picks up where the last one left off. If a system is nearing the end of its life, you will hear about it a season or two ahead, not in the middle of a heat wave. See tune-ups and maintenance.

Questions we hear

HVAC questions from New Albany homeowners

Do you serve all of New Albany?

We primarily serve the Licking County side of New Albany and the surrounding area. Call (740) 527-0222 and we will confirm service for your address.

Can you service homes with two or three HVAC systems?

Yes. We service multi-system and zoned homes regularly and can service every system in one visit.

Why is one zone in my house always uncomfortable?

Common causes include a failed zone damper, a thermostat issue, ductwork in a hot attic, or a system sized for the wrong load. We diagnose the whole zoning setup, not just the equipment.

Do you work on variable-speed and communicating systems?

Yes. We work on high-efficiency and communicating equipment from the major manufacturers and use their diagnostic tools and codes.

How often should an attic air handler be serviced?

At least once a year, with the condensate drain, secondary pan and float switch checked every time. A clogged attic drain can damage ceilings below.
