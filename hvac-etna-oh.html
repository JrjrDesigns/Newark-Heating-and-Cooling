Heating and cooling for Etna homes

Etna Township sits in the southwest corner of Licking County, where US 40 and I-70 cross, and it has changed more in twenty years than most of the county changes in fifty. Warehouses and distribution centers brought jobs, and subdivisions followed: street after street of two-story homes built from the early 2000s on, mixed with the older houses along the National Road and rural properties to the north and south of it.

What we see in Etna

  • Builder-grade furnaces and air conditioners from the 2000s and 2010s boom. These systems were built to a price, and 15 to 20 years is exactly when they start failing.
  • Undersized or leaky ductwork in fast-built homes, which shows up as one hot bedroom upstairs every summer.
  • Older homes along Route 40 with the same add-on ductwork challenges we see across the county.
  • New all-electric builds where a heat pump is the whole heating system, and correct setup matters far more than homeowners are told.
From the field

Whole streets in Etna were built within a year or two of each other, which means their furnaces age together too. When we replace a failed blower motor or cracked igniter in one of these subdivisions, we often end up back on the same street within the month, because the neighbors’ systems came off the same truck the same summer. If your home was built in the mid-2000s and the original furnace is still in it, this is the decade it gives out.

Repair or replace in a growing township

Because so many Etna systems hit end-of-life together, the repair-or-replace question comes up here constantly. Our honest rule of thumb lives in the repair-or-replace guide: a cheap fix on a 10-year-old system is usually worth it; a major repair on an 18-year-old builder-grade furnace usually is not. Either way you get the numbers first, from the same price ranges we publish.

From the field

Attic ductwork is the quiet problem in a lot of newer Etna two-stories. The system is fine; the ducts feeding the upstairs run through a 130-degree attic all summer. Sealing and insulating those runs, or adding a single return upstairs, has fixed more “my AC can’t keep up” calls here than any part we have ever replaced.

Getting an Etna home through winter

  • If your furnace is original to a 2000s build, have it tuned up before the first cold snap; heat exchangers and igniters from that era are on borrowed time.
  • Check that the furnace filter is not a restrictive 1-inch pleat jammed into an undersized return. It is a common cause of short cycling in these homes.
  • In all-electric homes, know where the auxiliary heat setting is on your thermostat, and call us if the aux heat runs constantly. That is a heat pump problem, not a weather problem.

Questions we hear

HVAC questions from Etna homeowners

My home was built in the mid-2000s and still has the original furnace. Should I be worried?

Not worried, but prepared. Builder-grade systems from that era commonly fail between 15 and 20 years. A fall tune-up tells you honestly where yours stands, and if it is near the end you can replace it on your schedule instead of during a cold snap.

One upstairs bedroom is always hot in summer and cold in winter. Is that the AC?

Usually it is the ductwork, not the equipment. Fast-built two-stories often have long, leaky or undersized duct runs to the far bedrooms. Sealing ducts, balancing dampers or adding a return usually fixes it, and it costs far less than a new system.

Do you serve the rural roads outside the subdivisions?

Yes. Etna Township is more than its subdivisions, and we cover the older homes and rural properties throughout it, including propane-heated ones.

Can you come the same day? I work at one of the warehouses and get home late.

Usually, and we will give you a realistic window when you call. Emergencies are answered 24/7 at (740) 527-0222, so a late call still reaches a person.

Are the new all-electric homes in Etna really cheaper to heat?

They can be, if the heat pump is set up correctly. A cold-climate heat pump with the right balance point runs efficiently through most of an Ohio winter. Set up wrong, the electric backup heat runs constantly and the bills show it. We service and correct both.
