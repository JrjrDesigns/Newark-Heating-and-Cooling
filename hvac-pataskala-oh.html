Heating and cooling for Pataskala homes

Pataskala has been one of the fastest-growing places in Licking County for years, with new subdivisions filling in along Route 16 and Route 310 and around the older village center. Most of its homes were built in the last twenty-five years, and that shapes almost every call we run here.

What we see in Pataskala homes

  • Builder-grade furnaces and air conditioners hitting 15 to 25 years old, often in the same subdivision at the same time.
  • Undersized return air. Builders frequently installed one or two central returns for a whole two-story house.
  • A hot upstairs in summer and a cool one in winter, from a single system serving both floors.
  • Bonus rooms over garages that never feel right in any season.
  • Condensate drains and pumps in finished basements, where a failed pump can do real damage.
From the field

In the early-2000s subdivisions around Pataskala, we sometimes replace furnaces on three or four houses on the same street within a couple of winters. They went in the same summer, they are the same model, and they are wearing out on the same schedule. If your neighbor just replaced theirs, it is worth having yours checked before the cold arrives.

Fixing comfort, not just equipment

When a Pataskala homeowner tells us the upstairs is always five degrees off, the answer is rarely a bigger system. It is usually return air, duct balance or a system that was oversized to begin with. Replacement time is the best chance to fix it: we size with a load calculation, add returns where the house needs them, and consider two-stage or variable-speed equipment that runs longer, gentler cycles and evens out the floors.

From the field

Finished basements are common in Pataskala, and so are condensate pumps hidden behind a wall or under the stairs. When one fails, water from the furnace or AC ends up in the carpet. We add a safety switch that shuts the system off before the pump overflows. It is a small part that prevents an expensive mess.

New construction around Pataskala and Etna

If you bought a newly built home in Pataskala or the Etna area, the HVAC system is covered by the builder and manufacturer warranties, but it is still worth a look in the first year or two. We often find thermostats set up with default settings, condensate lines without traps, and flex duct in the attic with sharp bends that choke airflow to the far rooms. None of these are expensive to correct, and catching them early protects the equipment.

Registering your equipment with the manufacturer, usually within 60 to 90 days of installation, can extend the parts warranty. Keep your paperwork and service records together; you will want them if a part fails down the road. See tune-ups and maintenance.

Questions we hear

HVAC questions from Pataskala homeowners

My Pataskala furnace is 20 years old. Should I replace it?

Not automatically. If it is safe and reliable, keep it and maintain it. If it needs a major repair, get a replacement price first. Our repair or replace guide explains the math.

Why is my upstairs so hot in summer?

Heat rises, attics bake, and many two-story homes have too little return air upstairs. Adding a return, balancing dampers or moving to a variable-speed system usually helps more than a bigger AC.

Do you serve Etna and the western edge of the county?

Yes. We serve Pataskala, Etna Township and the surrounding western Licking County area.

How much does a new furnace and AC cost in Pataskala?

A combined replacement typically runs about $7,000 to $13,000 depending on size and efficiency. See the cost guide.

Can you add a return vent without tearing up my walls?

Often, yes. In many two-story homes we can run a new return through a closet, a chase or an interior wall with minimal drywall work.
