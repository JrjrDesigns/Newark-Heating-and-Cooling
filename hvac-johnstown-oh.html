Heating and cooling for Johnstown homes

Johnstown sits in the northwest corner of Licking County where US 62 and State Route 37 meet. For a long time it was a quiet village surrounded by farmland. With major industrial development in the western part of the county, new housing has been going up around it, and the area now has a mix of older village homes, brand-new construction and rural properties.

What we see around Johnstown

  • Older village homes with aging gas furnaces and air conditioning added decades after the house was built.
  • Farmhouses on propane or fuel oil, sometimes with a furnace older than the people living there.
  • New construction with builder-grade systems that were installed quickly and sometimes sized by rule of thumb.
  • Well-water homes where the condensate from a high-efficiency furnace needs a drain plan that does not rely on a municipal sewer.
From the field

Out in the farmland around Johnstown, we still find oil furnaces in old farmhouses, and propane furnaces where the owners converted years ago. When those owners are ready for something new, we compare propane, a cold-climate heat pump and a dual-fuel setup on real operating costs. For a lot of rural homes, the heat pump comes out ahead.

New homes deserve a second look

A new house does not guarantee a well-designed HVAC system. In fast-built subdivisions, we sometimes find equipment that was sized by square footage instead of a load calculation, ductwork with crushed flex runs in the attic, or a single thermostat trying to control two very different floors. If your new Johnstown-area home has one room that never feels right, the fix is often simple once someone actually measures the airflow.

From the field

On newer two-story homes north of Johnstown, the most common comfort complaint we hear is a bonus room or upstairs bedroom that runs hot all summer. More often than not, the flex duct feeding it in the attic is kinked or pinched where it drops through the framing. Straightening and supporting that one run can change the room.

Condensate on country properties

High-efficiency furnaces and air conditioners produce water, and in town it simply goes to a floor drain. On rural properties around Johnstown with septic systems and no basement floor drain, that water needs a plan. Most homes use a small condensate pump that sends it to a sump, a laundry drain or outside. Furnace condensate is mildly acidic, so we often add a neutralizer where it drains toward plumbing or a septic system.

A failed condensate pump is one of the most common reasons a high-efficiency furnace quits in the middle of winter, so we check it on every visit and add a safety switch that shuts the system off before water overflows. See our furnace troubleshooting guide.

Questions we hear

HVAC questions from Johnstown homeowners

Do you serve the rural areas around Johnstown?

Yes. We serve Johnstown, Jersey and Monroe Townships and the surrounding northwest Licking County area, including homes on propane, oil and well water.

My new home is only a few years old. Why is one room so uncomfortable?

Usually ductwork: a kinked or crushed flex duct, too little return air, or a room over a garage or attic. We measure airflow to find the cause before recommending any equipment.

Can you replace an oil furnace?

Yes. We can replace an oil furnace with a propane furnace, a heat pump or a dual-fuel system, and we will compare the operating costs with you.

Is a heat pump a good choice for a propane home?

Often, yes. Propane costs more per unit of heat than natural gas, so an efficient heat pump, sometimes paired with a propane furnace for the coldest days, can lower heating costs significantly.
