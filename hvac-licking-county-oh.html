One HVAC company for the whole county

Licking County covers close to 700 square miles, and the homes change a lot as you drive across it. Newark and Granville hold some of the oldest housing stock in central Ohio. Pataskala, Etna and the western townships have added thousands of new homes in twenty years. North of Utica and east of Hanover, natural gas lines thin out and propane tanks sit beside farmhouses. We work across all of it, every week, and we plan each visit around what that part of the county is likely to throw at us.

How heating changes across the county

  • Newark, Heath and the older villages: natural gas furnaces in homes that have been remodeled two or three times, with ductwork added long after the house was built.
  • The western growth corridor around Pataskala and Etna: builder-grade furnaces and air conditioners from the 2000s and 2010s now reaching the age where they fail.
  • The rural north and east: propane and electric heat, long drives, and homeowners who cannot afford to wait days for a no-heat call.
  • Buckeye Lake: cottages that became year-round homes, with heating systems added into buildings never designed for them.
From the field

The same week can put us in a 1900s house near downtown Newark in the morning and a 2018 build in a Pataskala subdivision that afternoon. The two calls have almost nothing in common. One is about coaxing heat through a century of remodels; the other is usually a builder-grade part that gave out right on schedule. Knowing which problem belongs to which part of the county is half the job.

Cooling is the same story in reverse. Lake cottages fight humidity, brick ranches in Heath hold heat into the evening, and the big new two-stories out west struggle to push cool air upstairs. The equipment matters less than knowing which of those problems your house has, and that is what a company that works the whole county brings to the driveway.

What "county-wide" actually means for you

Plenty of companies list every town in the county on their website and then quietly charge trip fees or push far-out addresses to the end of the week. We schedule the county as one territory:

  • No-heat and no-cooling emergencies come first, wherever they are. A furnace out in St. Louisville matters as much as one in Newark.
  • Repairs are priced the same across the county. The quote depends on the fix, not the drive.
  • We group visits by area, so a tune-up in Hanover or Kirkersville books nearly as fast as one in town.
From the field

A stretch of Route 40 out past Hebron taught us to keep every common furnace part on the truck. When a home is twenty minutes from the shop, driving back for an igniter turns a one-hour repair into an afternoon. Stocking the trucks for the far ends of the county is the difference between fixing it today and fixing it tomorrow.

Questions we hear

HVAC questions from Licking County homeowners

Do you charge extra to come to the far side of the county?

No. The price of a repair depends on the repair, not the address. We schedule the county as one territory and stock our trucks so the distance does not slow the fix down.

Which parts of Licking County do you cover?

All of it: Newark, Heath, Granville, Pataskala, Hebron, Johnstown, Utica, Buckeye Lake, the Licking County side of New Albany, and the villages and townships between them, including Alexandria, Etna, Hanover, Kirkersville, St. Louisville, Jacksontown and Summit Station.

Do you work on propane and electric systems, or only natural gas?

All three. Natural gas dominates in the towns, but a large share of the county heats with propane or electric, and we repair and replace all of them, including heat pumps and dual-fuel setups.

Can you really get to a rural address the same day?

Usually, yes. No-heat calls are triaged first county-wide, and because we group work by area we are often already close. Call (740) 527-0222 and we will tell you honestly when we can be there.

Do you service all furnace and AC brands?

Yes. Carrier, Trane, Lennox, Goodman, Rheem, Bryant, Amana and the rest. Parts for common brands ride on the truck; anything else we can normally get within a day.
