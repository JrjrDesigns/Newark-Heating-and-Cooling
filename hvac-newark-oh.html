Heating and cooling for Newark homes

Newark is the county seat and our home base, and its housing covers more than a century of building. The older neighborhoods around downtown are full of two- and three-story homes built long before central air existed, many with basements that have seen coal, then oil, then gas. Farther out, ranch homes from the 1950s through 1970s and newer subdivisions bring their own mix of equipment and problems. We work on all of it.

Because we are based here, most Newark calls are a short drive, and on cold nights that matters.

What we see in older Newark homes

  • Furnaces venting into old masonry chimneys. When an 80% furnace replaced an older unit without a chimney liner, the flue gases can condense inside the chimney and deteriorate it. We check draft and the chimney base on every older home.
  • Ductwork that was added, not designed. Upstairs bedrooms fed by a single small duct squeezed into an interior wall, often with no return air.
  • Air conditioning retrofitted decades later, with line sets running through window wells and coils that do not match the furnace.
  • Asbestos-era duct wrap and pipe insulation in some basements. We identify it and work around it safely, and tell you when it needs professional abatement.
From the field

In a lot of the old homes near downtown Newark, the furnace sits in a stone or brick basement that has been damp for a hundred years. We see rusted cabinet bottoms and blower housings that corroded from underneath long before the furnace itself wore out. Setting the new furnace on a raised pad keeps it out of the water that shows up every spring.

Newark winters are hard on equipment

Newark sits where the North and South Forks of the Licking River join, and the river valley holds cold air on still winter nights. The first real cold snap of the season always brings a wave of calls, mostly from furnaces with worn igniters, dirty flame sensors and clogged condensate drains that were fine in October. A fall tune-up is the easiest way to stay off that list.

From the field

When the ice storms come through and knock out power across the east side of the county, we get a second wave of calls after the power returns: furnaces that will not restart because a surge took out the control board. We carry universal boards on the truck so a Newark family is not waiting days on a part.

Replacing a furnace in an older Newark home

Replacement in an older Newark house takes more planning than in a newer home. Basement stairs are often narrow and steep, and some basements were never meant to hold a modern furnace and coil stacked together. Venting is the other big question. A high-efficiency furnace vents through PVC pipe out the side of the house, which frees the old chimney, but on tight city lots the vent has to be placed carefully away from windows, doors and the neighbor's property. If you keep an 80% furnace on the chimney, the chimney usually needs a liner sized for it.

We work through all of that on the estimate visit so there are no surprises on installation day, and we pull the city mechanical permit. See furnace replacement for how the process works.

HVAC questions from Newark homeowners

How fast can you get to my home in Newark?

Newark is our home base, so most calls are a short drive. Same-day service is typical, and no-heat emergencies are answered 24/7 at (740) 527-0222.

Do you need a permit to replace a furnace in Newark?

Yes. Furnace and air conditioner replacements inside Newark city limits require a mechanical permit through the city. We pull it and schedule the inspection.

My old Newark house has no ductwork upstairs. What are my options?

Ductless mini-splits are often the cleanest answer for older homes, especially for upstairs bedrooms. In some homes we can add ductwork through closets or chases instead.

Do you work on old furnaces and boilers?

We repair older furnaces of any age as long as they can be made safe, and we will tell you honestly when a repair is not worth the money.

What does furnace repair cost in Newark?

Most common repairs run about $150 to $600. We quote the price before any work begins. See our cost guide for typical ranges.
